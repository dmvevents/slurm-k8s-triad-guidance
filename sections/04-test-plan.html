The live test plan not yet run

Everything on this site so far is desk analysis. These three arms are what convert it into measured fact. They are designed and gated, not executed — each needs a free 2×p5en pair, and this page will carry the numbers once they exist. Publishing the plan before the results is deliberate: it fixes the gates in advance so the numbers cannot be chosen after the fact.

T1 — NCCL at scale

all_reduce_perf -b 8 -e 8G -f 2 -g 8 across 2× p5en.48xlarge (H200, efa=16/worker), same public nccl-tests image under every arm so the number is comparable.

Gate: the launcher log must show NET/OFI ... Selected Provider is efa (not Socket/TCP), and busbw ≥ 350 GB/s @ 8 GiB.

Known trap: the three-bug playbook for public-image MPIJobs on fresh EKS — sshd StrictModes, ORTE routing, HPC-X UCC segfault. Set NCCL_NVLS_ENABLE=0.

SUNK is likely a SKIP at perf grade — its open recipe has no EFA, so a SUNK arm would be a TCP correctness run, not a comparable bandwidth number. Running it anyway and labelling it TCP is honest; presenting it beside EFA numbers would not be.

T2 — small-training communication scalability

An identical tiny GPT (~125M, torch DDP or a Megatron micro-config) on each arm: 1 node, then 2 nodes, fixed global batch. Measure step time, the DDP all-reduce fraction (torch profiler), and 1→2 node scaling efficiency.

Why this arm exists: a raw all_reduce_perf number tells you the fabric works; it does not tell you what a scheduler's placement and plumbing cost a real training step. Same container and EFA env across arms, so the measured delta isolates the scheduler layer (Slinky srun vs ours vs K8s-native).

Gate: comm fraction must be consistent with T1's busbw — if they disagree, one of the two measurements is wrong and neither gets published.

T3 — dynamic scale, live

Slinky: NodeSet replicas: N↔0, HPA-driven scaling, and a Karpenter node addition — measuring scale-up latency at both layers separately (pod seconds vs node minutes). SUNK: pod layer only, as shipped. Ours: documents the "no" — static slurm.conf node list is the blocker, and that is a finding, not a failure to test.

Also worth measuring: whether drain-before-terminate actually protects a running job on scale-in. That is the operationally decisive behaviour from page 03.

Capacity discipline (why this is gated)

The bake-off needs a genuinely free pair of p5en nodes. "Free" means 0 GPUs allocated computed from pod specs — not a low pod count, which is how you accidentally evict someone. Live tenants are never preempted; the cluster lock is claimed before any deploy and released after, with teardown to zero idle spend. At the time of writing only one p5en is free, so all three arms wait.
